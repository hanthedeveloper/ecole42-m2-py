1 After validators run after Pydantic’s internal parsing.
They are generally more type safe and thus easier to implement.
2 Before validators run before Pydantic’s internal parsing and validation
(e.g. coercion of a str to an int).
These are more flexible than After validators since they can modify the raw input,
but they also have to deal with the raw input, which in theory could be
any arbitrary object.
3 Plain validators are like a mode='before' validator but they terminate 
validation immediately, no further validators are called and Pydantic does not 
do any of its internal validation.
4 Wrap validators are the most flexible of all. You can run code before or after 
Pydantic and other validators do their thing or you can terminate validation 
immediately, both with a successful value or an error.

rec bir sözlük (dict) olduğu için. * ve ** farklı şeyleri açar.
* : listeyi/tuple’ı açar (sadece değerler)

def topla(a, b):
    return a + b
sayilar = [3, 5]
topla(*sayilar)      # topla(3, 5) ile aynı

* sadece değerleri sırayla verir. İsim bilgisi yok, sıra önemli.
** : sözlüğü açar (isim=değer çiftleri)
python
bilgi = {"a": 3, "b": 5}
topla(**bilgi)       # topla(a=3, b=5) ile aynı

** sözlükteki her anahtarı parametre adı, her değeri o parametrenin değeri yapar.
args = [1, 2, 3]
f(*args)   # f(1, 2, 3)

kwargs = {"a": 1, "b": 2}
f(**kwargs)  # f(a=1, b=2)
